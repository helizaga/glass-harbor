moombahton / global club

night-drive menace, clipped swagger, hot concrete, vocal attitude, crowd-first pressure

## BPM

## Key / Scale

## Style Lane

Moombahton Club

## Accent

global-club / DJ Snake-adjacent

## Structure

- intro: 4
- groove: 8
- lift: 4
- breakdown: 4
- return: 8
- second-return: 8
- outro: 4

## Hook Identity

- primary hook carrier: `vocal_chop` sliced into a short slogan-like phrase
- supporting answer: `bass_pitched` answers the vocal instead of sitting under every hit

## Low-End Plan

- what owns the sub / bass pocket: `bass_pitched`
- how should kick and bass trade space: kick stays dry and punchy on anchors; bass hits in the gaps and short answers

## What To Withhold Until Return

- save the heavier `impact_wide`, denser percussion, and stronger bass-answer pattern for the first return

## Breakdown Subtraction Plan

- remove: most kick density, most hats, most bass movement
- keep: one clipped vocal fragment, a skeletal percussion cue, and a single threatening downbeat
- first thing to re-enter: kick and vocal slogan, then the full bass answer

## Sourced Truths

- he explicitly does not want to be stuck in one style and draws from many cultures without anchoring to one formula
- melodies matter to him more than lyrical comprehension, so the hook still needs to be immediate even when vocal material is fragmentary
- his beat-making process starts from a melodic trigger and then moves toward knocking drums
- in "Lean On", the harmonic bed is very repetitive while drums, vocal textures, and timbral changes carry the section contrast
- the kick can stay lean if the sub owns the floor underneath it
- swung detail, recycled drum colors, and treated vocal textures are part of what keeps a simple loop alive

## Musical Inferences

- keep the harmonic material minimal and let rhythm plus texture define the record
- make the main hook a processed vocal fragment, not a long topline
- let the bass answer the hook and the kick instead of sustaining through the whole groove
- use percussion and drum-pattern changes to create section contrast
- keep transitions sharp and functional instead of washing them in pad bloom

## Capability Gaps

- the runtime pack can match the drum grammar and pacing better than a signature DJ Snake vocal or timbral finish
- current vocal chops can do attitude punctuation but not elite topline identity
- this draft can aim for the lane honestly, but not claim exact commercial authenticity

## Sonic Goals

- dry, heavy, chest-forward drums
- one obvious vocal slogan hook
- lean bass answers with minimal overlap
- no airy pad wash
- no loud default synth lead

## Review Goals

- preserve kick clarity
- keep the groove syncopated instead of house-straight
- make the return hit harder through re-entry, not extra layers
