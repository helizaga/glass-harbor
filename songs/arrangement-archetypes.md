# Arrangement Archetypes

Use these as hidden scaffolds for first drafts. They are not templates to copy literally.

## Patient Bloom
- `intro` or `anchor`: sparse bed, one hook hint, low rhythmic pressure
- `groove`: pocket settles before adding width
- `lift`: brightness and percussion motion rise, harmony still restrained
- `breath`: deeper subtraction than feels comfortable on paper
- `return`: wider and heavier than the lift, but with fewer simultaneous ideas

## Percussion Reveal
- start with texture and low-end pulse
- reveal percussion in layers instead of dropping hats immediately
- keep one distinctive percussion lane as the motion driver
- let the return win through groove identity, not just density

## Subtractive Breakdown
- remove kick support or bass sustain before the return
- keep only one emotional anchor in the break
- avoid stacking risers and pads at the same time
- make the first downbeat back feel obvious

## Strong Return
- return should reintroduce one clear element at the first downbeat
- do not add every lane back at once
- protect kick/bass pocket before widening the mix
- let contrast come from weight and spacing, not clutter

## Filter-House Loop Climb
- loop-first groove with low harmonic drift
- timbral movement does more work than new notes
- use filter opening, mute choreography, and top-line punctuation
- keep the pocket locked while brightness rises
