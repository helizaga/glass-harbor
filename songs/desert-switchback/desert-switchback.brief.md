# Desert Switchback

## Genre

festival trap / moombahton crossover

## Mood

hot-night street pressure, swagger, clipped vocal attitude, desert-stadium tension

## BPM

102

## Key / Scale

F minor

## Structure

- intro: 4
- groove: 8
- lift: 4
- breakdown: 4
- return: 8
- second-return: 8
- outro: 4

## Hook Identity

- primary hook carrier: `vocal_chop` answer phrase framed by a short `pluck_pitched` call
- supporting answer: short `stab_tonal` brass-like punctuation in the returns

## Low-End Plan

- what owns the sub / bass pocket: `bass_tonal` owns the weight with sparse, chest-level hits
- how should kick and bass trade space: kick stays dry and obvious on the anchors; bass answers in the gaps instead of riding under every hit

## What To Withhold Until Return

- save the full vocal punctuation lane and the wider stab punctuation for the first return

## Breakdown Subtraction Plan

- remove: most hats, most percussion motion, most bass activity
- keep: a single hook fragment, air bed, and occasional downbeat kick
- first thing to re-enter: the kick and vocal answer before the wider stab punctuation

## Sourced Truths

- the lane is club-first and impact-driven, not pad-led
- syncopated reggaeton or moombahton pull matters more than straight four-on-the-floor flow
- drums and bass should feel blunt and body-first
- vocals often work best as punctuating hook material rather than a full sung topline
- contrast and subtraction are a bigger part of the payoff than constant layer growth

## Musical Inferences

- keep the kick and clap dry and obvious
- let bass answer the kick instead of sustaining under every hit
- use vocal chops as the main attitude hook and let pluck phrases set them up
- make the breakdown a stripped frame, not an emotional pad valley
- stage the return with re-entry and punctuation instead of supersaw bloom

## Capability Gaps

- current vocal chops are good for punctuation but too generic for signature topline character
- runtime pack can match the drum grammar better than true artist-specific timbre
- this draft can capture pacing and pocket more convincingly than elite commercial finish

## Sonic Goals

- keep the drums dry, sharp, and chest-forward
- make the groove feel tense and syncopated instead of straight-house
- use `vocal_chop` as the main attitude hook, not a sung topline
- let `pluck_pitched` set up the vocal answer instead of trying to carry the whole record
- avoid overly bright supersaw energy or big-room pad wash

## Review Goals

- preserve the kick and bass pocket
- make the return feel bigger through re-entry staging, not clutter
- keep the top end clean while the percussion still feels aggressive
